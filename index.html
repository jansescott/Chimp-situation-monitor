Situation Report — Uganda Chimp Conflict

Updated: April 2025 • Source: Field research synthesis & published literature

THREAT LEVEL
HIGH
🔴 NGOGO COMMUNITY

With ~220 individuals, Ngogo remains the world's largest documented chimpanzee community. Territorial expansion campaigns begun in 2013 have netted the group an estimated +22% land area, now totalling ~41 km². Patrol frequency remains elevated at the northern and southern borders as the community probes potential new expansion targets.

EXPANDING HIGH PATROL RATE
🟠 KANYAWARA COMMUNITY

The smaller Kanyawara group (~50 individuals) faces growing existential pressure along its shared border with Ngogo. Territory has contracted ~12% since 2013. Researchers recorded 7 incursions by Ngogo males into Kanyawara range in 2024 — a 40% increase on the 2023 baseline. Female emigration to neighbouring groups has been observed.

UNDER PRESSURE TERRITORY LOSS
🟢 BUDONGO FOREST

The Sonso (~85 individuals) and Waibira (~80 individuals) communities in Budongo Forest Reserve remain relatively stable. The Budongo Conservation Field Station (Univ. of St Andrews) continues long-term monitoring. A 2024 study documented novel tool-use behaviour — moss-sponging to collect water — spreading culturally between communities, the first cross-group cultural transmission confirmed at this site.

STABLE ACTIVE RESEARCH
⚡ EXTERNAL PRESSURES

Extended dry seasons driven by the 2023–2024 El Niño have reduced fruit availability in forest margins across western Uganda, forcing communities to range further — increasing inter-group contact probability by an estimated 30%. Illegal logging along the Kibale–Maramagambo corridor continues despite protected status. Uganda Wildlife Authority expanded its camera-trap network to 120 devices across Kibale in early 2025.
